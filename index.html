What does warmth mean here?

The shaded band is the stretch of weeks when both of you were warm at the same time, so it is set by whoever was cooler. The lines cross whenever one of you was a little warmer than the other, and a crossing by itself means nothing. Warmth is a score for each person, each week, built from four things in the chat: how much they wrote compared with their usual, how quickly they answered, how often they showed affection or humour, and how often they asked about the other person. It reads behaviour in messages. It cannot read what someone felt.

What this can and cannot tell you

It describes the past. It does not predict.

Research on couples' messages finds that balance, responsiveness and matching style go with relationships that last. Those are averages over many couples. For one relationship, the chat explains what changed and when far better than it forecasts anything.

A chat cannot prove a reason.

Half of a relationship happens in person and on calls. A theme with a high score means it shows up often in the messages. It is never proof, and for infidelity in particular a chat on its own cannot confirm or rule it out.

The other person is in this file too.

The export holds someone else's private messages. Keep the report to yourself, and do not use it to confront, monitor or expose them.

WhatHappened is a prototype. It is not counselling or advice. If a breakup is weighing on you heavily, talking to someone you trust or a professional will help more than a chart.
